# Level 4 — Flood maps of Banteay Meanchey, October 2020

**Date:** 23 September 2026
**Models:** all under `evaluation_plan.md` v1.1 (RTC γ⁰ pipeline, the same inputs they were trained on)
**Scenes:** Sentinel-1 RTC, track 164 descending — 26 Sep (pre), 14 Oct (peak), 1 Nov (post). Radar covered **100 % of the province on all three dates**.
**Grid:** EPSG:32648, 20 m, 4,949 × 6,009 px; province area computes to 6,148 km² against the official 6,146 km².
**Scripts:** `scripts/level4_cambodia.py` (maps + areas), `scripts/level5_change_detection.py` (flood = peak water minus pre-event water), `scripts/plot_level4.py` (figures)
**Artefacts:** `results/level4/areas.{csv,json}`, `change_detection.{csv,json}`, `maps/*.tif`
**Figures:** `docs/figures/level4_flood_map.png`, `level4_timeseries.png`

**Nothing here is scored.** Tier B hand labels do not exist yet, so these are maps and areas, not accuracy numbers. The labelling grid is fixed (§4) so scoring can follow without any choice being made after seeing model output.

## 1. Flood extent

Flood = detected water on the date **and not** water on 26 Sep, on the same track and the same processing.

| Method | Water 26 Sep | **Flood 14 Oct** | of which cropland | Still flooded 1 Nov | Drained by 1 Nov |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VH threshold (Level 1) | 111 km² | **882 km²** | 815 km² | 823 km² | 361 km² |
| U-Net VV only | 70 km² | **1,131 km²** | 1,013 km² | 1,108 km² | 371 km² |
| U-Net VV+VH cropland-weighted (selected) | 230 km² | **1,218 km²** | 1,113 km² | 1,072 km² | 365 km² |

All three agree on the spatial pattern (`level4_flood_map.png`): flooding concentrated in the southern and south-eastern lowlands along the Mongkol Borei river toward the Tonle Sap floodplain, with a reservoir in the north-east. The threshold map is speckled; the U-Net maps are contiguous. **The three methods differ by 36 % in total extent (882 → 1,218 km²), and without hand labels there is no basis to say which is closest.**

## 2. Tier D — permanent water is almost useless here

JRC Global Surface Water (occurrence ≥ 80 %) gives only **10 km² of permanent water in the whole province** — 0.16 % of its area. Banteay Meanchey has no large permanent lake; the Tonle Sap lies in neighbouring provinces.

Consequence: subtracting permanent water, as `areas.json` does, removes almost nothing (e.g. 965 → 965 km² for the threshold at peak). **Tier D cannot separate flood from seasonal water in this province.** The pre-event subtraction in §1 does the work instead, which is why the change-detection step was brought forward from Level 5.

## 3. Tier E — the sanity check does *not* pass cleanly

Reported for the province (`data/reference/tier_e_reference.json`): **28,248 ha of rice inundated = 282 km²**.

| Method | Flood on cropland at peak | Ratio to the report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VH threshold | 815 km² | **2.9 ×** |
| U-Net VV only | 1,013 km² | **3.6 ×** |
| U-Net VV+VH cropw (selected) | 1,113 km² | **3.9 ×** |

The plan's rule for Tier E is "catch absurd results". A factor of 3–4 is not absurd — it is the same order of magnitude — but it is too large to wave through. Four candidate explanations, and what would settle each:

1. **Different quantities.** The report may count rice *damaged* or *destroyed*, assessed per district, while the satellite measures every pixel under water on one morning. Settling this needs the underlying NCDM assessment, not more processing.
2. **Normal wet-season water.** In October a transplanted paddy is deliberately ponded. 26 Sep is only 18 days before the peak and was nearly dry (70–230 km²), so the *increase* is real — but that does not prove the peak state is abnormal. **The decisive test is the same track and dates in a normal year** (14 Oct 2019 and 14 Oct 2021). If those show a similar extent, most of what we map is ordinary paddy water and only the excess is flood. This is the single most valuable next measurement and it is cheap.
3. **Over-detection.** Level 3 measured this: the selected model has ~4 × the cropland false positives of the VV-only U-Net. Consistent with it giving the largest extent here. Tier B labels settle it.
4. **Mis-stratification.** WorldCover "cropland" is not "rice"; some flooded non-rice cropland is counted. Small effect, but real.

Note the direction of the disagreement: the model that scored best on the benchmark's flooded-vegetation task also produces the largest, least conservative Cambodia map. That is exactly the trade-off recorded at Level 3, now visible on real ground.

## 4. Tier B labelling grid — fixed and ready

`data/labels/tiles.geojson`, generated by `scripts/level4_select_tiles.py` from 1,417 candidate 2 × 2 km tiles that lie inside the province and have radar on all three dates:

| Stratum | Tiles | Composition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cropland | 12 | mean 100 % cropland |
| vegetation | 3 | mean 99 % tree/grass/wetland |
| built | 3 | mean 49 % built-up (Sisophon and towns) |
| water | 2 | mean 43 % open water, 41 % JRC permanent |

20 tiles, 80 km². Selected by land-type fraction with an 8 km minimum separation, ordered by a fixed shuffle (seed 0) so the labeller does not work through one stratum at a time. **No model output was used in the selection** — otherwise the labels would depend on the thing they are meant to test. Tile outlines are drawn on `level4_flood_map.png`.

The protocol (`data/labels/LABELLING_PROTOCOL.md`) is still **draft v0.1**. It must be read and frozen before the first polygon is drawn, and its evidence hierarchy is the important part: label from optical and hydrological evidence, and only use radar to refine a boundary that optical has already established. Labelling from radar darkness would rebuild the exact bias the project exists to measure.
